# Stage 2 — Inverse Dynamics + Static Optimization: Results & the Fatigue Finding

Generated by `Code/run_id_so.py` (OpenSim 4.5.1, `biomech` env) driving
`Model/arm26_paper_loaded_brd_elbow_research.osim` with
`Data/paper_minjerk_fatigue_10cycles.mot`.

## Method (why ID + SO, not CMC)
- **ID** → net joint torques (label + sanity check).
- **SO** → per-muscle activation & force (the core internal labels).
- Chosen because the task is **cyclic** (Anderson & Pandy 2001: SO ≈ dynamic
  optimization), SO is the only method that **scales** to a multi-subject dataset,
  and its cost (min Σ activation²) is Crowninshield & Brand's (1981) fatigue proxy.
  CMC is reserved for a small validation subset (activation dynamics; far slower).
- **Kinematics low-passed at 6 Hz** before ID/SO — removes 6–12 Hz tremor (which
  would inflate torques/activations) while keeping the curl and fatigue trends.
- **Elbow reserve actuator weakened to 1 N·m** so muscles carry the elbow load
  (Hicks 2015: reserves must stay small).

## ⚠️ The key fatigue finding

Per-rep flexor activation across the 10 cycles:

```
rep   peak elbow Nm   mean flexor activation (sum)
 1        8.8                 0.435
 5       10.1                 0.443
10       10.6                 0.450      (+3% only)
```

**Mean muscle activation is essentially flat across reps (+3 %).** The peak
activations rise modestly, but that is driven by *kinematic drift changing the
torque demand*, not by a loss of force capacity — and it is non-monotonic (a
drift artifact), not a clean fatigue trend.

This confirms the research conclusion: **Stage-1 kinematic degradation alone does
NOT reproduce the physiological fatigue signature.** Real fatiguing curls show
aEMG rising substantially and MPF falling 25–29 % (Potvin 1997; Potvin & Bent
2010) — i.e. **activation rises because capacity falls, for the same demand.**
Kinematic fatigue actually *reduces* demand (smaller ROM, slower), so SO activation
stays flat or even drops.

**Therefore, to produce a correct fatigue label you must add the 3CC layer:**
integrate the Xia–Frey-Law (2008) three-compartment model with the elbow-specific
F/R rates (Frey-Law et al. 2012), and **re-run SO with each muscle's peak isometric
force scaled by its available fraction `MA/(MA+MF+MR)`** (the post-hoc coupling of
§2.2.8; Carbone et al. 2023). As capacity declines, the same demand forces rising
activation → the Potvin/Bent signature. This gives two complementary signals:
- **observable (vision):** slowing / reduced ROM / tremor (Stage-1),
- **internal (label):** rising activation + declining capacity (3CC + SO).
